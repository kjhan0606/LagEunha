# Independent evaluation of the t10 thermal64 comparison

Astra evaluated the completed same-parent controls. The disk reduction of approximately 3.02 million is strong evidence of sensitivity to the thermal64 arithmetic package, which changes authoritative U/V, EOS, RHS and RK4 anchoring together. It is not isolation of U storage alone or proof that float accumulation is the only mechanism. The frozen K1 rounding result is a mechanism indicator, not the fraction of final RK4 updates lost or a long-time bias measurement.

The core entropy improvement is modest, approximately 1.6 times, and does not repair the core density/orbit error already present at t10. An almost constant entropy and a small short-restart energy drift do not independently establish physical trajectory accuracy or long-time conservation. Single-rank, one wall-impact and short rollback tests do not prove multi-rank or event convergence.

The review recommends measuring the t10 thermal initialization on the same H200 because the quoted ~2e-11 disk residual is below the previously observed cross-host geometry-check scale; comparing an uncapped thermal64 control with the existing capped one to determine whether the costly cap is needed; then advancing the new arithmetic from a preserved early accepted checkpoint while comparing core and disk separately to the existing baseline. If the core fails by t6.5, investigate force, geometry and time integration before extending. If validated, advance through t10 then t120 and t600, with GIZMO units, IC, boundary and diagnostics aligned. A real MPI migration/restart gate is required before claiming multi-rank capability.
