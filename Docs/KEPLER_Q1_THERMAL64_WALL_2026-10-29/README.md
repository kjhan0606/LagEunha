# Experimental thermal64 bounded-wall transaction

The separate candidate builds on the RK4 anchor fix and uses the existing wall transaction for a one-rank, fixed-mass, W0 thermal64 trial. It checks authoritative U64/V64/tag/anchor and RHS where applicable, sums U64 in the wall energy ledger, and checks the trial before the final EOS update. Whole-particle rollback and cache invalidation remain those of the existing wall controller. The periodic transaction remains a distinct, mutually exclusive path. The three patches apply on top of the anchor-fix candidate; their application is reproduced by the build script.

The later [endpoint audit](THERMAL64_TAGGED_RESTART_AUDIT.json) independently rereads the already saved tagged thermal64 restart at t=.0016 and its t=.00167 endpoint. It confirms tagged initial U is used in total energy, fixed mass/W0, positive tagged endpoint U/V, float mirrors and EOS consistency, and the one-step wall angular ledger. Relative total-energy change is -3.32e-16; no wall impact occurs. This is a reader and accounting check on an existing one-step restart, not a new evolution test or evidence for long-time stability.

On Ada, an accepted native checkpoint at t=0.0015284035789018555 was advanced to t=0.0016. A verification rejection was injected once. The native log reports exact full-payload/ledger restoration and device cache invalidation; its second attempt was accepted with no impacts. Independent checkpoint inspection confirms 90,352 identical IDs and masses, W0, valid tagged thermal state, float mirrors and EOS. The independently recomputed total energy changed by -3.32e-16 relative to its initial magnitude, and no floor or dual-energy injection was reported. The endpoint, source and original log hashes are in `SMOKE_AUDIT.json`.

The no-rejection control advanced the same accepted state with the same step to t=0.0016. Its entire 576-byte record except the first eight-byte linked-list pointer, and its entire restart footer, exactly match the forced-rejection result for all 90,352 particles (`ROLLBACK_CONTROL.json`). A second test ran two steps continuously to t=0.00167 and compared this to a fresh process restarted from the no-rejection t=0.0016 checkpoint. The two step sizes and resulting non-pointer record bytes and footer match exactly (`RESTART_CONTROL.json`). The files have different raw SHA-256 hashes because the linked-list pointers differ. This tests a real restart, but only on this short no-impact trajectory.

An additional actual impact replay started from the preserved late Q2 t=48.37534965270793 preimpact checkpoint and ended at t=48.378. With thermal64 enabled, the original ID 55283 struck facet 543 at t=48.377024019249085; the impact gap was 9.44e-16, below its 2.30e-14 tolerance. Four accepted prefixes and eight retries completed, all rollback checks passed, and the independently recomputed total energy using U64 changed by -4.61e-14 relative to initial magnitude. The largest per-prefix momentum and angular residuals were 5.70e-18 and 4.18e-15. All declared checks, including fixed mass, W0, positivity, EOS, no energy injection and inside-domain endpoints, passed (`IMPACT_AUDIT.json`). The raw checkpoint and log remain in `/gpfs/kjhan/LagForce/Kepler_Thermal64WallImpact_20261029/`. This is a single local Q2 event, with no event-order convergence or Q1 stability claim.

The candidate is not qualified for production. In particular, adverse-state handling including EOS overflow, rollback after different failure stages, impact timestep convergence, full energy/work audits, MPI scaling and long-time Kepler behavior still require tests. It does not establish a solution to the pre-existing Q1 core instability. The existing H200 and Ada calculations continue with the untouched legacy executable.
