# Next numerical direction after the capped reference

Astra assessed the observed core orbit error and global-step cost. The proposed next engineering candidate keeps the current spatial forces but splits the fast central-gravity evolution from slower pressure updates, integrating both positions and velocities during gravitational microsteps. This changes the time integrator; it is not the existing classical RK4 algorithm. A repeated kick at frozen positions does not solve the orbit problem.

Before such a method can be promoted, its discrete pressure force and internal-energy work must use consistent geometry, states and time quadrature. The conservation identity sum(v·F_pressure)+sum(dU/dt)=0 must be tested for the closed inviscid system; gravity error must never be transferred to internal energy to conceal it. If the mesh or neighbor set changes fast, pressure is not necessarily a slow term. Independently refine macro and micro steps, comparing core and disk density, entropy, total energy, angular momentum and actual wall impulse against the fully capped native reference. Measure actual GPU/MPI/OpenMP cost.

Collision detection must use the actual modified microtrajectory and rollback the complete macrostep. The existing RK4 cubic event predictor cannot simply be reused for a different trajectory. For future Laguerre weights, volume depends on both positions and weights and their work terms must be included; zero weights must recover fixed-mass Voronoi. Re=infinity and no steering remain requirements.

Individual block timesteps are the broader long-term option, but active–inactive faces require common-time states, paired impulse/work, neighbor wakeup, MPI halo synchronization, complete rollback and an angular-momentum budget. Antisymmetric pair momentum alone does not guarantee angular conservation for noncentral face contributions. Shocks require entropy production and timestep synchronization even when physical viscosity vanishes. A smooth Kepler result cannot substitute for those tests.

The new bounded Q1 t10 native K1 and full interior reconstruction provide a concrete counterexample to assuming thermal work is slow just because pressure acceleration is weak: for original r0<.1, the mass mean of abs(Udot)/(U Omega) is .236 and the cell p99 is .889. The disk mass mean is .00349. This is an instantaneous measurement in an already inaccurate core and is not entropy production.
